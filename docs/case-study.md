# The Bay Outlook - Project Case Study

## Problem

Economic information about the Bay Area is fragmented across agencies, geographies, methods, and release schedules. Comparing counties responsibly requires more than collecting numbers into one table.

## Solution

A tested system that preserves raw releases, standardizes official data, validates provenance and geography, builds reviewed comparisons, and makes freshness and evidence gaps public.

## My role

Defined the research framework, designed the data model, built ingestion and validation pipelines, created the analytical and editorial layers, designed the dashboard, and wrote the baseline economic analysis.

## Actual tools

Python, SQLite, SQL, GitHub Actions, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Matplotlib, ReportLab, source-data charting, and reproducible economic research methods.

## Result

A public nine-county dashboard supported by responsible automation, immutable evidence, visible limitations, and a publication system that reserves interpretation and approval for named humans.

## Verified scale

- 9 Bay Area counties
- 25 cataloged indicators
- 891 verified observations
- 83 automated tests at the Phase 11 handoff

## What I learned

“Current” is a source-specific condition. Monthly labor data, quarterly establishment data, and annual GDP cannot be treated as one synchronous snapshot, and missing coverage is itself important information.
