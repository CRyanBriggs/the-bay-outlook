# Limitations

- HCD APR data are jurisdiction-reported administrative records and can be revised. A permit does not prove construction start; a completion record does not prove occupancy.
- Application, entitlement, permit, and completion counts are annual stage flows, not one tracked cohort. The release intentionally produces no stage-conversion funnel.
- Same-year completion-to-permit flow percentages can exceed 100 percent because completions may correspond to permits issued in earlier years.
- Timeline matches cover only records with exact jurisdiction and tracking ID or APN. Unmatched projects are not assumed to have missing or slower outcomes, so the medians are not necessarily representative of all applications.
- Tracking IDs and APNs can be incomplete or reused in source records. Exact keys reduce ambiguity but do not prove a one-to-one lifecycle in every case.
- Application decision shares use records with an explicit approved or disapproved status; pending, withdrawn, and blank statuses are outside that denominator.
- Housing-element compliance is a current administrative status that can change after retrieval. It does not measure implementation, production, zoning legality, or development feasibility.
- RHNA is a full-cycle planning allocation, while the permit and completion numerators cover only 2023–2025. Progress is not a forecast of cycle-end attainment.
- The statewide zoning layer is a compiled, generalized map with varying local vintages and classifications. Area shares are not legal capacity, allowed density, parcel feasibility, or unit potential.
- Table C rezoning fields are conditionally reported. Absent rows are not converted to zero and should not be treated as proof that no rezoning occurred.
- No descriptive component proves a policy's causal effect. Version 1.3 intentionally produces no county ranking or policy score.
